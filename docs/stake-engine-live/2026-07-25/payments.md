# payments

Payments

Stake Engine offers two ways to get paid for your games. You pick your payment model per team and can switch between them at any time from the Payments page — a change takes effect from the following month.

In the app	Payment model	Rate
Profit Share	10% GGR (Revenue Share)	10% of actual GGR
Guaranteed	7.5% Guaranteed Payment	7.5% of expected GGR

Throughout this page, GGR (Gross Gaming Revenue) is:

GGR = Total Bets − Total Wins Paid to Players

Option 1: 10% GGR (Revenue Share)

With this option, you earn 10% of your game’s Gross Gaming Revenue (GGR).

If your game performs well (players lose more than expected), your GGR is higher and so is your payout.
If players get lucky and your game has negative GGR, you do not owe Stake any money. Instead, a negative balance (debt) is recorded and carried forward.
Example
Month	GGR	Your 10%	Running balance
January	−$10,000	−$1,000	−$1,000 (debt)
February	+$6,000	+$600	−$400 (debt reduces)
March	+$8,000	+$800	$400 clears debt, you receive $400

In March, $400 of your $800 clears the remaining debt and you receive the other $400.

Key points
You never pay Stake money out of pocket.
A negative balance carries forward indefinitely until future positive earnings offset it.
You don’t receive payouts until the debt has been cleared.
There is no time limit on carrying the balance forward.
This model has higher upside, but also more variance.
Option 2: 7.5% Guaranteed Payment

With this option, you earn 7.5%, but it’s calculated using your game’s expected RTP rather than the actual results.

Your payment is based on what the game is expected to make mathematically.
If players get lucky, your payment doesn’t decrease.
If players get unlucky, your payment doesn’t increase.
There is no negative balance and no debt — Stake takes on all of the volatility.

Note: If you carried a negative balance (debt) from a previous 10% GGR period when you switch to 7.5%, that existing debt still needs to be earned off first. The difference is that your balance can now only go up — your 7.5% payments go toward clearing the debt and no new debt can accrue while you’re on this model.

Example

If your game is mathematically expected to generate $10,000 in GGR for a period:

Under the 7.5% model, you’d receive $750, regardless of whether the actual GGR ended up being $15,000 or −$5,000.
Under the 10% GGR model, your payout would depend entirely on the actual GGR achieved.
Which should you choose?
10% GGR (Profit Share)
Higher long-term earning potential.
More volatile.
Negative months create a carry-forward balance.
Better if you’re comfortable with variance and believe your game will perform well over time.
7.5% Guaranteed
Stable and predictable income.
No debt or carry-forward balance.
Lower upside, but zero risk from player variance.
Good if you prefer consistent payments.
Recommended for teams getting started (Especially if you have bills to pay)

In short, the choice is between taking a share of the actual results (10% GGR) or accepting a slightly smaller but guaranteed share based on expected performance (7.5%). The important distinction is that under the 10% model, the only risk is delayed payouts due to a carry-forward balance — you are never required to pay Stake if your game has a negative month.

How and when you’re paid
Add a wallet on your team settings — one wallet per team.
Payouts run on the 1st of every month. A paid invoice appears first, then funds are sent within 12 hours.
We pay out any amount above $0.00 — even $0.10.
