## NOTES

**What section 4 actually holds up to.** The whole of section 4's path surface is live: nineteen
named paths, nineteen present. The failures are not dead paths, they are two evidence claims
where the path resolves and the CONTENT does not support the claim (D03-01, D03-02). Both are
of the shape this audit exists to catch, and neither is visible to a reference scan, because a
scan sees the path and stops.

**On section 8 versus section 9.** The 8/9 split works. Section 8's header is dated, 9e corrects
five of its claims by name, and every 9e correction I tested was right. The residual risk is the
claims 9e does NOT touch, and D03-02 is the one that matters: 8c's provenance for the RTP band
and the outcome cap is wrong, it is a number and a citation a reader would quote to the platform,
and 9e passes over it. D03-03 is the mirror-image problem: 9e is presented as current, and one of
its own corrections has since gone stale twice over.

**Two things I deliberately did not claim.** The seven maths files' SHA-256 values were not
re-derived (D03-07), and the logo's alpha channel was not pixel-read (D03-06); both are stated
as untested rather than folded into a pass. Separately, `scripts/validate_math.py` carries STATED
constants that differ from the dossier's figures (`cruise_std 11.10`, `super_std 500.0 # approx`
at lines 43-45) - I checked before reporting it and did NOT open a finding, because `near(a, b,
rel)` at line 179 is RELATIVE, so the 0.05 tolerance is 5 per cent and 11.29 against 11.10 passes
comfortably. That would have been a false finding of exactly the shape the preamble warns about.
