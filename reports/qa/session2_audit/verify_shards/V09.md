### S2-C015, REQ-127, win readouts rounded to two decimals

Both code quotes are verbatim at HEAD. `currency.ts:211` and `:218-219` are exactly as cited,
and `WinPod.svelte:14-15` reads `formatBalance(Math.round($winAmount * CURRENCY_SCALE), $currencyCode || 'USD')`.
The no-proof half also survives: `currency.test.ts` uses its own `eq()` harness and carries no
assertion about win precision, and `win_countup_steady_gate.mjs` (checks.yml:583-584) is a jitter
gate. I could not refute the observation.

I refute its SCOPE, and the exemplar is the weak end of the class rather than the strong end.
`WinPod`'s only consumer in the tree is `ReplayMode.svelte:19` and `:310`. It is a replay surface,
not an in-game readout, so the one component the finding names is the instance least covered by a
requirement whose words are "wins in-game show exact win amounts". The real in-game readout is
`HudOverlay.svelte:366`, which feeds `data-testid="hud-win"` on all four layout profiles
(`:391`, `:607`, `:688`, `:868`) through the same two-decimal formatter, and it is uncited. The
rounding site is also wider than the cited range: `formatBalance` clamps to `decimals` in three
separate branches, `currency.ts:191-192` (platform display metadata), `:202-203` (virtual
currencies) and `:218-219` (fiat), so a fix at 211-220 alone would leave the sweepstakes and
platform-metadata paths rounding.

Severity is over-pitched. The platform text at `docs/stake-engine-live/2026-07-29/rgs.md:297`
reads, verbatim: "How these win values are displayed is at the discrecion of the publisher, though
it is reccomonded that the extra precions is only displayed with the base bet-size is <$0.10 ...
and it is only a requirement that wins in-game show exact win amounts." The requirement sentence is
real, so this is not nothing; but the same paragraph confines the recommendation to base bets under
$0.10, and our exposure is the narrow band where a win lands on a third decimal (bet 0.10 with the
0.08x per-way L3 value gives $0.008 displayed as $0.01). A player can see it, it overstates rather
than understates, and it is a MEDIUM, not a headline.

### S2-C017, REQ-045, browser-default focus ring and scaffold hover colour

Verbatim at `app.css:157-163`. This one I tried hardest to refute and it got stronger instead.

The obvious refutation is that components override it. Four files carry a focus style
(`BetSelector.svelte:283`, `HudOverlay.svelte:1057`, `ResumeOffer.svelte:125`,
`FreeSpinsPresentation.svelte`), and each is written against `:focus-visible` only:
`.bet-open:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--sig-cyan, #00FFFF); }`,
`.bs-level:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--sig-cyan); }`. `:focus-visible` does not match a
mouse click on a button, and `button:focus` at `app.css:160` does. So on mouse interaction, which is
how a reviewer will drive this game, every one of the roughly 90 buttons in the tree gets the
scaffold rule, INCLUDING the four that have a designed focus state. The single `outline: none` at
`HudOverlay.svelte:1720` is on a range-input track (`appearance: none; height: 4px`), not a button.
No gate covers this: `machine_tell_gate.mjs` is wired three ways in checks.yml (`:287` `--self-test`,
`:291` `--source`, `:462`) and its classes are font stacks, glyph iconography and dashes, with nothing
for a scaffold colour or a default focus ring.

One correction to the reasoning, and it matters for the fix. The claim says "Chrome's own
`-webkit-focus-ring-color` `rgb(0, 95, 204)`, so a stock browser blue ring". `rgb(0, 95, 204)` is the
WebKit value; and because `outline-style` here is `auto`, Blink paints its own default ring and
ignores both the declared `4px` and the declared colour. The tell is therefore "the browser's default
focus ring", not "a stock blue". The load-bearing inference, a browser default standing in where a
designed focus state should be, on the most-touched controls, is sound and is exactly what REQ-045
names ("border effects standing in for real art"). Fix by replacing the rule, not by recolouring it.

MEDIUM rather than HIGH: it is one thin ring on a control the player has just clicked, it persists
until focus moves, and it is genuinely visible, but it is not a headline surface defect.
