## THE STRUCTURAL FINDING ABOUT THIS AUDIT'S OWN SHAPE, recorded first because it changes the method

**Clustering achieved almost nothing here: 315 findings collapsed to 311 clusters, and
CROSS-SQUAD corroboration is ZERO.** That is not a marshalling failure, it is a property of
how the wave was designed, and it is worth writing down because the method document currently
implies the opposite.

Session 1 clustered 540 findings into about 40 defects, a 13x reduction, and
`docs/skills/AGENT_BUDGET_AND_SCHEDULING.md` section 4 recommends cluster verification as
"the default" on the strength of it. **That reduction came from OVERLAP**: 26 squads all
examined the same 26 frames through different lenses, so the same defect was reported many
times and verifying it once settled all of them.

**This session partitioned its 41 squads into DISJOINT scopes by design.** Each of the 23
walk squads held different requirements; each of the 10 census squads a different directory;
each of the 8 currency squads different documents. Nothing overlapped, so nothing corroborated,
so there was nothing for clustering to collapse.

The consequence is a budget one, and it was caught by computing the verification cost at
launch rather than discovering it afterwards, which is what convention rule 15 exists for:

| Policy | Verifiers | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Per cluster, single vote, as the method recommends | 311 | 29.5M |
| Per cluster, 3-vote on STREAM and single elsewhere | 353 | 33.5M |
| **Batched: several clusters per verifier, grouped by surface** | **~23** | **~2.2M** |

Against roughly 5.0M of usable budget remaining at that moment, the first two DO NOT FIT and
the third does. **So the lesson generalises: cluster verification is cheap only when the
discovery wave OVERLAPPED. For a partitioned wave, the lever is BATCHING several findings per
verifier, not clustering.** Both reduce verifier count; they are not the same mechanism and
they do not compose.
