## PER REQUIREMENT NOTES

**The single fact behind six of the seven rows.** `.github/workflows/checks.yml` contains
**zero** occurrences of `tile`, `brand`, `delivery`, `kit`, `logo` or `provider` (grep over
the whole file, no hits). The submission tile set at `design-system/brand/delivery/` is
therefore outside CI entirely: no gate, and none of the 20 test files touches it. Every
asset in that directory is correct today and undefended tomorrow. The three scripts that
produce or consume it (`frontend/scripts/tile_delivery_build.mjs`,
`frontend/scripts/provider_logo_derivation.mjs`, `scripts/kit_build.mjs`) all WRITE, so
none of them was run for this audit; all three were read as text.

**REQ-169 (foreground is high resolution PNG with a transparent background).** The
delivered file is genuinely PNG RGBA at 4159x1875 and is a byte copy of
`tile_hero_full.png` (both 433,497 bytes on disk, and `README.md:27` names that source).
`GENERATION_NOTE_hero.md:59` states "Both tight alpha-cropped, transparent background". The
only captured output is `reports/screens/brand-tile-hero/gate_output.txt`, seven lines from
the 2026-07-23 hero build, whose `[PASS] fringe gate (hero): 0 offending pixels` concerns
alpha EDGE quality on the master rather than the delivered copy's format. It is a log, not
an instrument: it cannot go red later. `tile_delivery_build.mjs` was read in full (217
lines) and its only failing conditions are a missing source (`process.exit(1)` at `:110`)
and the 3 MB ceiling (`:214-215`); it never inspects a PNG signature or an alpha channel,
so even wiring it into CI would not close this row. What I could not establish: whether any
job anywhere re-verifies the delivery set's formats. I looked in `checks.yml`, `scripts/qa/`
(two files only: `bet_level_compliance.py` and `locked_paths_gate.mjs`), and the tile and
brand scripts under `frontend/scripts/`.

**REQ-170 and REQ-173 (the two naming conventions).** Both names are exactly right, and
`README.md:9-12` tabulates each delivered name against the platform rule it satisfies,
quoted from the dated mirror. The nearest thing to a defence is `scripts/kit_build.mjs:305-307`,
which `cpSync`s the three names literally, so a rename would crash the kit build with
ENOENT. That is a real failure mode but it is SCRIPT_UNWIRED: `kit_build.mjs` appears
nowhere in `checks.yml`, it is run by hand at upload time, and it writes. Also worth
recording: that list is three files, so `FutureSpinner-Tile.png` (the 408x546 composed
tile the delivery README tells the owner to use if the editor wants a single image) is NOT
copied into `03_branding` by the kit build. That is a possible gap in the kit rather than
in REQ-170 or REQ-173, and I did not chase it far enough to call it: flagged, not claimed.

**REQ-171 (the studio's official provider logo, not the game logo).** Satisfied on the
evidence: the delivered mark originates from a file the owner supplied
(`PROVENANCE_f.md:9`, "Transparent We Roll Spinners icon.png"), the studio is We Roll
Spinners and the game is Future Spinner, and `PROVENANCE_f.md:35` records "There is no
wordmark", so it cannot be a wordmark variant. `README.md:60-63` records the adoption
instruction ("Go with F", 2026-07-26). What has no defence is the distinction itself. The
tree holds both `frontend/public/assets/ui/logo_future_spinner.png` (game) and
`logo_we_roll_spinners.png` (studio), and nothing checks which one reaches
`delivery/WeRollSpinners-Logo.png`; `provider_logo_derivation.mjs:298-302` re-hashes the
copy against its chosen master, which detects a corrupt copy but not a wrong choice of
master. `REVIEW_TRACKER.md:130` (TR-031) is the row for this work and it reads
"**MERGED. Candidate f ADOPTED 2026-07-26; only the one-time portal upload remains, and
that is the owner's**", i.e. it is a CLOSED row, so it is a record of satisfaction and not
a proof path under this audit's rubric.

**REQ-172 (provider logo is high resolution PNG with a transparent background).** Measured
and true: `file` reports 1024x1024 8-bit RGBA, and `PROVENANCE_f.md:19` gives the exact
alpha census "531,237 opaque, **0 partial**, 517,339 clear". Both are one-off
measurements. `PROVENANCE_f.md:31-33` also records WHY this matters here specifically:
candidate e "had no alpha at all and we had to key its field out", so the project has
already shipped a candidate that failed this clause, which is precisely the case a gate
would exist to catch. Nothing catches it now.

**REQ-174 (the logo stays clear and legible at small sizes).** This is the strongest
implementation evidence in the slice and still has no proof path, and the reason is
structural rather than an oversight of mine: `provider_logo_derivation.mjs` computes a
RELATIVE verdict. `:216-226` counts, per size, how many of three measures each candidate
wins, and `:230` adopts the winner. A comparison has no floor. If both candidates were
illegible the script would still adopt one and still print a verdict, and there is no
threshold anywhere for any of the five measures. So the committed claim "candidate F wins
at 32px, 3 of 3 measures" is a ranking, not a pass. **One measured figure in the same
document argues this row is not merely unproven but partly open**: `invisibleInk` is
defined at `PROVIDER_LOGO_DERIVATION.md:52` as "share of opaque ink below a 1.5:1
contrast ratio against the portal surface: ink present in the file and not visible on the
page", and candidate f reads 24.9% at 128px, 22.5% at 48px and 13.7% at 32px against the
real portal surface `#1d1d1d`. Roughly a quarter of the mark's ink is measurably invisible
where the platform actually draws it, which the document itself anticipated at `:42`,
recording f's "open risk that its structural colour `#0A0A14` is opaque near-black and can
dissolve on a dark surface". I am NOT calling this NOT_MET: the derivation's own conclusion is that f is
the most legible asset the project holds, the 32px decision size is where invisibleInk is
lowest, and the judgement belongs to the owner and to Fable per convention (l.8). I am
recording it as the measured concern that sits under this requirement, severity HIGH
because the provider mark is player-visible in the lobby and because a submission art
review is exactly where it would be raised.

**REQ-175 (tile pixel geometry is not stated in the capture).** The slice's platform text
is UNKNOWN by construction, and I confirmed the absence rather than assuming it: I read
the whole 2026-07-29 capture (`approval_guidelines_game_tile_requirements.md`, 1,341 chars,
sha256 `08f7a9e0...`), and it contains no pixel dimension, no aspect ratio, no minimum
resolution, no safe area and no colour space. The only size-adjacent text is "High
resolution PNG or JPG file", "High resolution PNG with a transparent background" twice, and
"Please ensure that the background & foreground images don't exceed more than 3MB
combined". The repository behaves correctly against this: the one geometry figure it uses,
408x546, is sourced to a survey of live published assets
(`published-tile-geometry.md:48`, 81 of 87 decoded headers, 93.1%) and both the delivery
README and `tile_delivery_build.mjs` label it so: `:69` is
`rule: 'no published rule, matches observed published geometry',` and the comment above it
at `:58` reads "the docs, which give no dimensions at all". The requirement is a
documentation-discipline one, so a genuine proof path would be a check that no document
attributes a geometry to the platform docs, and no such check exists; `COMPLIANCE_WATCH.md`
and convention (d) are manual. Recorded NO_PROOF at LOW rather than N_A, because the
practice IS the implementation and it is real, opened and quoted.
