# Session Report - R122 POSE-CHANGING STRIP INTAKE: the idle finally changes pose, and the two reaction strips are refused because the hero's arms are cut off at the edge of their own canvas (2026-08-26)

**MORNING SUMMARY.**

1. **ONE OF THREE SHIPPED. The idle now genuinely changes pose** - a planted-foot lateral weight
   shift, arms staying crossed. Silhouette motion **0.278% (R120) to 1.007% (R121) to 1.866%**.
2. **TWO REFUSED, AND NOT ON A METRIC.** `win-reaction-unfold` and `feature-brace` have the hero's
   **arms severed by the edge of their own canvas** - 192 pixels of flat-cut opaque limb on one
   frame. The missing pixels do not exist in the source, so nothing downstream can recover them.
3. **The package's QA is not wrong, it is measuring something else.** Its three published figures
   reproduce to within 0.7% once its convention is identified - MAX XOR against rest, normalised
   by BODY pixels. R121's floors were MEAN change between CONSECUTIVE frames over FRAME area.
4. **dist went DOWN, 23.47 MB to 22.69 MB**, because the accepted sheet is 685 KB smaller than the
   one it replaces. Headroom is now 2.31 MB, better than when the session started.
5. Their acceptance suite tests identity, rest-return, ground stability and silhouette change.
   **None of those can see a limb cut off at the frame boundary.**

## 2. RECONCILING THE PACKAGE'S NUMBERS, WHICH ARE NOT WRONG

The package reports idle 10.742%, win 23.194%, feature 24.599%. I measure 8.13%, 9.17%, 9.60% as
maxima. The gap is not error, it is convention, and it reproduces exactly:

| strip | package claims | XOR vs rest, normalised by BODY pixels | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| idle-weight-shift | 10.742% | 10.778% | 1.003 |
| win-reaction-unfold | 23.194% | 23.353% | 1.007 |
| feature-brace | 24.599% | 24.706% | 1.004 |

**They measure the maximum XOR against the REST frame, normalised by body pixels.** R121's floors
were written as mean change between CONSECUTIVE frames, normalised by frame area. Both are
legitimate; they are not interchangeable, and theirs is the more generous.

**A frame-count-independent read**, because mean-per-pair shrinks when the same motion is split over
more frames:

| | frames | total silhouette path |
|---|---|---|
| LIVE idle | 5 | 2.05% |
| LIVE win | 8 | 2.28% |
| LIVE energy | 7 | 2.61% |
| NEW idle-weight-shift | 6 | **16.89%** |
| NEW win-unfold | 8 | **28.48%** |
| NEW feature-brace | 7 | **32.35%** |

All three carry 8x to 12x the motion of what they would replace. **On motion alone, all three pass.**

## 3. WHY TWO ARE REFUSED ANYWAY

**THE HERO'S ARMS ARE CUT OFF BY THE EDGE OF THE CANVAS.** Measured as fully opaque pixels sitting
in the first or last column of the 680x1344 frame:

| strip | frame | left edge | right edge | span |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| win-reaction-unfold | 3 | **192px opaque** | 0 | y336-527 |
| win-reaction-unfold | 4 | **176px opaque** | 0 | y346-534 |
| win-reaction-unfold | 5 | 0 | 33px | y531-563 |
| win-reaction-unfold | 6 | 0 | 24px | y509-532 |
| feature-brace | 4 | **51px** | **56px** | y531-631 |
| idle-weight-shift | all | 0 | 0 | clear |

A 192-pixel run of fully opaque material lying flat against the boundary is not a glow tip, it is a
severed limb. Inspected at 2x zoom, the raised forearm's tube simply ends in a hard vertical cut
where the canvas does. At render scale that becomes a ~58px flat edge on a 407px figure, at stage
x=22, against the scene - **visible, and it would read as a rendering fault rather than a
performance.**

**Nothing downstream can fix it.** The pixels were painted past the canvas and lost. Packing cannot
restore them. Insetting the figure onto a wider canvas only moves the flat cut inboard, which is
worse - an amputated arm floating in clear space instead of one that ends at the frame.

**The package's own acceptance suite cannot see this.** It tests identity, first-frame-equals-last,
ground-line stability and silhouette change. All four pass on both refused strips. **Edge contact is
simply not among the things it looks at**, which is why re-measuring first-hand was the brief's
first workstream.

## 4. WHAT SHIPPED

`hero_crossed_idle_6f.png`, 6 frames, packed at 0.58 common scale, 394x780 per frame,
**1,402,693 bytes replacing a 2,087,774-byte sheet: net -685,081 bytes.**

Sanitation: RGB zeroed where alpha is 0 **before and after** the resample. Before-only is not
enough - a windowed-sinc resampler mixes opaque neighbours into pixels that end up transparent, and
the first attempt left a residual of 18. **The shipped sheet measures 0.**

**On the packing scale.** R117 packed at 0.70; this ships at 0.58, and that is a measured choice
rather than a budget concession. Rendering the same master down to each realistic target:

| render target | 0.70 source | 0.58 source | 0.50 source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 206x407 (1x stage) | 0.0020 | 0.0023 | 0.0028 |
| 309x610 (1.5x stage) | 0.0028 | 0.0042 | 0.0067 |
| 618x1221 (1.5x stage, 2x display) | 0.0093 | 0.0140 | 0.0179 |

Mean absolute error out of 255. Even at the most demanding target every option is two orders of
magnitude below visibility. **Source scale above 0.50 buys nothing here**, and 0.58 leaves 1.9x the
1x render size in each axis.

## 5. THE R121 TRANSFORM HAD TO BE RETUNED, AND THAT IS A MEASURED CALL

R121's idle sway existed because the frames could not move the figure. The new strip moves it
laterally, and a lateral rotation on top of a lateral weight shift is the R115 double-bob mistake on
a different axis. Measured:

| idle configuration | silhouette | excursion | head travel |
|---|---|---|---|
| new strip, no transform | 1.525% | 4.350% | 13.29px |
| new strip + the full R121 sway (1.05deg) | 1.545% | 7.025% | 18.74px |
| **new strip + a 0.32deg de-loop (SHIPPED)** | **1.866%** | **4.813%** | **14.99px** |

At the full R121 amplitude, per-sample silhouette change **fell** while excursion climbed - the
signature of two motions cancelling at some phases and stacking at others. Cut to 0.32deg all three
metrics rise together. It is kept rather than removed because the strip is a 6-frame loop on a 4.4s
cycle and a 7.2s rotation beats against it so the loop never lands the same way twice. **Its job is
now de-looping, not motion.**

## 7. TWO INSTRUMENT FAULTS OF MY OWN, BOTH CAUGHT

**I STEPPED ONE ANIMATION AND LET THE OTHER RUN FREE.** The transform lives on `.hero-body` and the
flipbook on `.hero-idle`. Pausing only the transform and setting its `currentTime` produced captures
that mixed a stepped pose with an arbitrary flipbook phase, and the chest-width trace came back
nonsense - starting at 205px (already unfolded) and ending at 0. Fixed by pausing and stepping both.

**MY CHEST-WIDTH METRIC HAD A CEILING I DID NOT NOTICE.** Measured inside a 206px-wide element
capture, any figure that overflows its box reads as exactly 206px regardless of pose. The in-game
"unfold" reading of 206px was the box edge, not the arms - which is what led me to check the source
canvas and find the severed limbs. **The broken metric found the real defect**, but only because
206.0 exactly is an implausible measurement rather than a plausible one.

## 8. THE EXACT RE-RENDER REQUEST

Two strips, re-rendered so the arms stay inside the frame. Everything else about them is already
correct and should not be changed - identity IoU 0.9930, first frame equals last, ground drift 0px,
and 12x the motion of the incumbents.

1. **`win-reaction-unfold`** - the same performance, with the canvas widened or the figure inset so
   that **no opaque pixel touches column 0 or column 679** on any frame. Frames 3 and 4 currently
   lose roughly 190px of the raised forearm; frames 5 and 6 lose 24-33px on the right.
2. **`feature-brace`** - the same, for frame 4, which loses ~51px on the left and ~56px on the right.

**The acceptance test to run before delivery** is the one that caught this, and it is one line of
intent: for every frame, the count of pixels with alpha > 8 in the first and last columns must be
**zero**. The existing suite's four tests all pass on the broken frames, so this needs to be added
rather than assumed.
