TWO METHOD RULES CARRIED FROM SESSION 1, both measured rather than argued:

UNKNOWN IS A RESPECTABLE ANSWER AND IS OFTEN THE CORRECT ONE. Session 1's panels found that only 5 of 26 clusters had a cause that survived a hostile read: the defects were real and the DIAGNOSES were mostly wrong. One shard's proposed fix was a no-op; another converted one visible defect into another. JOB 1b is a claim-a-path task and is exposed to exactly this failure. So: a claimed implementation path or proof path must be OPENED and QUOTED, never inferred from a filename, a grep hit or a plausible-looking module. If a squad cannot open it and quote it, the answer is UNKNOWN and that is a complete answer, not a failure. A confident wrong path is worse than an honest gap, because it closes a requirement that is actually open.

CLUSTER ACROSS ALL SEVERITY TIERS FIRST, FILTER BY SEVERITY AFTERWARDS. Session 1 clustered the STREAM tier alone and hid genuine corroboration twice, because two squads tier the same defect differently and the filter drops one of them. Also: a cluster's corroboration count from grep-level marshalling is a HYPOTHESIS, not evidence, until a panel tests it. Session 1's marshal fused two pairs of unrelated defects, counted two squads reading one image as two instances, and counted a signed WITHDRAWN DRAFT CLAIM as a corroboration. Label counts as provisional and let the panels settle them.
